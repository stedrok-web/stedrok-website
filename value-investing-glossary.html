Value Investing Glossary

This glossary explains the main terms used in the Stedrok framework in plain language. These are the same concepts the engine applies when it scans more than seven thousand companies twice each trading day.

See also: How the scoring works · Our investment philosophy

Free Cash Flow

Money a company has left after it pays for its normal running costs and needed investment. It is the cash that can be used to pay down debt, buy back shares, pay dividends or reinvest.

Free Cash Flow Yield

Free cash flow per share divided by the share price. It is a way to see how much cash the business generates for each dollar you pay for the stock.

Drawdown

The distance from a recent peak price to the current price, expressed as a percentage. Stedrok looks at drawdowns to understand whether a stock has pulled back to a level that might be interesting for a value investor.

Debt to Equity Ratio

Total debt divided by total equity on the balance sheet. A high ratio means the company uses a lot of borrowed money relative to what shareholders own. Stedrok treats high debt as a red flag for balance sheet risk.

Margin of Safety

The gap between what you pay for a stock and what you think it is worth. A large margin of safety protects you if your valuation is wrong or the business hits trouble.

Return on Equity (ROE)

Net income divided by shareholder equity. It measures how efficiently a company uses shareholder capital to generate profit. Higher ROE usually indicates better quality operations.

Price to Earnings Ratio (P/E)

Share price divided by earnings per share. A common valuation metric. Lower P/E can mean cheaper, though context matters.

Current Ratio

Current assets divided by current liabilities. Measures whether a company can pay its short term debts. A ratio below 1 means the company might struggle to meet near term obligations.
