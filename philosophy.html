Stedrok philosophy

Scan a broad universe. Apply a transparent value lens. Keep risk in view. Make the next decision simple. We scan thousands of companies twice daily, sanitise the inputs, and surface a shortlist of stronger, lower-risk ideas from available data.

What we look for

Value, not hype

We seek a clear discount to a conservative view of intrinsic worth, verified against multiple valuation anchors — not a single ratio taken in isolation.

Quality that endures

Healthy cash generation, sensible reinvestment, prudent capital allocation, and accounting that matches economics — quality that survives a cycle.

Resilience first

Balance-sheet strength and liquidity matter. We avoid fragile structures, serial diluters, and "cheap for a reason" profiles.

Market context

We want calm price behaviour relative to business value. Parabolic moves and crowding are red flags; quiet compounding is a feature.

Discipline over prediction.

We do not forecast markets. We run the same process every day and let the math do the boring work while you dollar-cost average into a few names.

Your two-minute check before buying

  1. Earnings and calendar: Check the next report date and any guidance changes.
  2. News: Scan for litigation, regulatory action, credit downgrades, or restatements.
  3. Liquidity: Confirm average daily turnover is adequate for your order size.
  4. Position sizing: Keep it small and repeatable — dollar-cost average over days or weeks.
  5. Broker ticket: Use limit orders when spreads are wide.

Why we care about index concentration

Market-cap weighting can morph "diversification" into a concentrated bet. The top companies in broad indexes can represent a surprisingly large share of the total value — levels that make stock selection and valuation discipline more, not less, relevant today. A value lens helps you avoid paying any price for growth and can help you keep fragile names out of the portfolio.

Concentration levels fluctuate. We highlight the risk to illustrate why a value process matters.

Dollar-cost averaging

Many long-term investors prefer steady amounts (daily or weekly) to reduce timing risk and decision stress. Research shows lump-sum often wins in rising markets, but dollar-cost averaging can improve investor experience by reducing regret and volatility. Use the approach that fits your temperament and stick with it.
